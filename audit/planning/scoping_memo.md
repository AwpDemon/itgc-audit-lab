# Planning and Scoping Memo - FY2026 ITGC Testing

**Client:** Kestrel Valve Co. *(fictional, ~70-employee valve manufacturer)*
**Period under audit:** 1 October 2025 - 30 September 2026 (fiscal year end 30 September)
**Prepared by:** Ali Askari (portfolio lab engagement) | **Status:** final for lab purposes, not reviewed by a manager

> Lab context: the company, its people and its tickets are synthetic and generated by
> `python -m itgc scenario`. The *systems* are real: a Samba 4 Active Directory domain
> controller, a PostgreSQL ERP database and a git/CI repository were built and seeded, and all
> system evidence was pulled from them live with read-only accounts.

## 1. Why ITGCs are in scope

The financial-statement auditors plan to rely on automated controls and system reports in the
ERP (three-way match, credit-limit check, the AP aging and GL trial-balance reports). That reliance
is only possible if the general IT controls around the ERP operate effectively for the whole
period: if anyone could change the program, or anyone could log in as anyone, the automated
controls and reports cannot be trusted. ITGCs are therefore tested first and their result feeds
the reliance decision.

## 2. In-scope systems (the "IT environment" walkthrough)

| Layer | System | Why in scope |
|---|---|---|
| Application | ERP (order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, GL) - `app.*` schema | Financially significant; processes all revenue, purchases and journals |
| Database | PostgreSQL 16 hosting the ERP | Direct DB access bypasses application controls |
| Operating system / directory | Active Directory domain `kestrel.lab.test` (Samba 4.17) | Authenticates every user; AD groups drive ERP roles via nightly sync |
| Change pipeline | `order-portal` git repository + CI/CD deployment log | Every program change reaches production through it |
| Operations | Backup scheduler, ERP-to-GL batch interface, ITSM incidents | Completeness and recoverability of financial data |

Out of scope: physical security, network perimeter, end-user computing (spreadsheets), third-party
service organizations (no SOC 1 reports needed - everything is on-premises), program development
(no new system implementations in FY2026).

## 3. Risk assessment and control selection

Risks were taken from the standard SOX ITGC domains and mapped to the company's controls in the
RCM (`audit/rcm/rcm.yaml`, also the `RCM` sheet of the workpapers). 15 controls were selected:
7 access, 4 change, 4 operations; 12 are key controls. Risk ratings (High / Moderate) drive sample
sizes. Every control is mapped to COBIT 2019, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 and the SOC 2 Trust Services
Criteria.

## 4. Approach

* **Criteria** - the client's own Information Security Standard ISS-04 v3.1
  (`audit/pbc/it_security_standard.yaml`), approved by the CFO before the period.
* **Evidence sources** - (a) *system-generated*, pulled by our scripts with read-only accounts:
  LDAPS (`svc_audit_ro`), PostgreSQL (`audit_ro` with `pg_read_all_data`), git; (b) *PBC*:
  HR roster, ITSM exports, review sign-offs, job logs. Every file is SHA-256 hashed in
  `manifest.json` at collection.
* **IPE (information produced by the entity)** - before using any population we checked its
  completeness and accuracy: CI/CD deployments were reconciled to git tags (32 = 32); AD accounts
  created in the period were reconciled to HR hires; the backup log was checked for one run per
  calendar day (365 = 365).
* **Testing method** - where the population is machine-readable, 100% data analytics; otherwise
  random samples sized from the frequency table (`src/itgc/sampling.py`), with reproducible seeds.
  For sampled controls we also ran the same attributes over the full population; deviations found
  outside the sample are evaluated (they cannot be ignored) and reported separately.
* **Inquiry** steps are documented in each workpaper but marked *not performed* - there is nobody
  to interview in a lab, and inquiry alone is never sufficient evidence anyway.
* **Design vs operating effectiveness** - one walkthrough per process (design) is implied by the
  procedure documentation; the tests themselves are tests of operating effectiveness across the
  period.
* **Deficiency evaluation** - rule set in `src/itgc/deficiency.py` (realised / pervasive /
  compensating controls / aggregation by risk), with the rationale written into the SAD sheet.

## 5. Known limitations (agreed up front)

* AD keeps no history of group membership or of when an account was disabled. Disable timing is
  therefore tested from ticket closure dates; current group membership is used as evidence of
  membership during the period.
* `lastLogonTimestamp` replicates only every 9-14 days, so dormancy uses a 14-day tolerance.
* `whenCreated` and `lastLogonTimestamp` were back-dated in the lab with the LDB *relax* control
  to simulate a year of history; `pwdLastSet` cannot be back-dated, so password-age testing is out
  of scope.
* The audit account cannot read fine-grained password policies (default AD ACLs). A PSO does exist
  (confirmed with a Domain Admin export during fieldwork); this is recorded as a scope limitation.
* Business days ignore public holidays.
